As part of my onboarding process for CUSail, I had to design a miniature sailboat to develop my CAD skills and better understand the design considerations that go into creating a stable sailboat hull. We even got to go out to the gorge and test our designs at the end of the semester!

I began with an initial design featuring a 240 mm long hull with a 51.34 mm beam width, a mast holder, fully covered top, and rudder. After completing the first version, I tested the mini boat in water to evaluate its stability and identify areas where the design could be improved.

<img src="{{ '/assets/images/MiniBoat1.png' | relative_url }}" width="500
">

Based on the results of this testing, I returned to the CAD model and made several significant design changes. I increased the beam width from 51.34 mm to 102.12 mm, nearly doubling the width of the hull to improve its stability in the water. I also added an additional support beam for the mast holder to create a more structurally supported mounting point. Finally, I designed a longer keel mount, allowing the keel to provide greater stabilization and help the boat remain upright.

<img src="{{ '/assets/images/MiniBoat2.png' | relative_url }}" width="500
">

This project introduced me to the iterative engineering design process, where physical testing directly informed changes to my CAD model. Rather than stopping after creating the initial design, I used the performance of the first prototype to identify weaknesses and redesign the hull, mast support, and keel system to produce a more stable final boat.
